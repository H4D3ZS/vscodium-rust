\begin{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{The question.} When a memory system replaces $n$ cluster
members with $m < n$ representatives, what geometry decides whether those
representatives still recover the members by retrieval?

\noindent\textbf{The law.} We prove a \emph{Consolidation--Interference
Duality}: for any consolidator, whenever the retrieval cap half-angle
$\thetap = 1-\theta$ is below the mean within-cluster cosine distance
$\dbar$, the identity error obeys
$\eps_{\mathrm{id}} \geq 1 - c_1\,m\,(\thetap/\dbar)^{\deff/2}$,
where $c_1$ is an absolute constant and $\deff$ is the local effective
(participation-ratio) dimension. The law separates a \emph{tight regime}
($\dbar < \thetap$, identity cheap) from a \emph{spread regime}
($\dbar \geq \thetap$, identity expensive).

\noindent\textbf{The surprising consequence.} Every real-text corpus we
measure sits in the tight regime the law declares safe, and the cheapest
consolidator there is the centroid. Across six corpora and six sentence
encoders, the \emph{fixed centroid} ties or beats every adaptive operator,
including the per-cluster router we built to probe the law. Centroid
dominance is not a heuristic; it is what the law predicts on real text.

\noindent\textbf{Evidence.} We validate the regime boundary on a
$400$-cell synthetic grid ($16{,}000$ trials): tight-regime cells achieve
mean cap-error $\leq 0.5\%$; spread-regime cells diverge to $30$--$74\%$
with an order predicted by $\deff$. We calibrate the bound empirically:
the tight-regime $c_1^{95} \approx 0.05$ on $12{,}400$ cells --- within
an order of magnitude of the ideal Berry--Esseen constant; in the spread
regime $c_1$ grows unbounded as $\theta \to 1$. On
retrieval-augmented Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct QA, centroid \emph{hurts} NQ by
$4.2$ EM, is neutral on HotpotQA, and \emph{wins} PopQA by $8.4$ EM over
GAC --- matching the cap-coverage prediction of which operator the law
prefers per corpus. We treat Geometry-Aware Consolidation (GAC) not as a
product but as the instrument that made the measurement.

\noindent\textbf{Scope.} Embedding consolidation in contrastive unit-norm
text spaces under cosine-threshold retrieval. The isotropic cap-volume
bound is shape-correct in the tight regime and loose-to-vacuous in the
extreme-coherence corner of the spread regime; an anisotropic refinement
is the central open theoretical problem. All $17{,}813$ experimental
cells are open-source under MIT license.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Introduction}

Memory systems that summarize old traces into representatives -- cognitive
consolidation\cite{mcclellandConsolidation1995,alvarezSquireConsolidation1994},
retrieval-augmented generation with vector-store compression\cite{lewisRAG2020},
replay buffers in continual learning\cite{robinsReplay1995} -- all rest on an
implicit hope: the important structure is low-dimensional, so a few
representatives can stand in for many traces without losing identity.
The previous two papers in this trilogy challenged that hope. The first showed
that production embedding spaces concentrate $\sim 99\%$ of their variance in
$\deff \approx 16$ effective dimensions\cite{geometryOfForgetting2026};
the second showed that \emph{any} kernel-threshold memory with finite $\deff$
forgets by a lower bound that no amount of storage can
evade\cite{priceOfMeaning2026}. Consolidation looks like a way out: rather
than raising capacity, we lower bookkeeping. In this paper we show that
consolidation shares the same geometric floor. The geometry that forces
forgetting also forces identity collapse under compression, by an analogue
of the same argument.

Beyond the theoretical statement, a conference-level evaluation requires that
we benchmark consolidation operators against the dominant vector-store
quantization family in production systems -- product
quantization\cite{jegouPQ2011}, optimized product
quantization\cite{ge2013optimized}, locality-sensitive
hashing\cite{charikarLSH2002}, PCA with int-8 coding, and HNSW with pruning.
We test six sentence-encoder families
(BGE-base/large\cite{xiao2023bge}, MPNet\cite{song2020mpnet},
MiniLM\cite{wang2020minilm}, E5-large\cite{wang2022e5},
Nomic\cite{nussbaum2024nomic}) to separate theorem-predicted universality
from encoder-specific artefacts, and we measure identity under
paraphrase queries to distinguish the bound's geometric regime from a
purely memorization regime.

% Contributions.
Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item[\textbf{(C1)}] \textbf{Consolidation--Interference Duality} --
    a theorem relating identity retrieval after compression to the same
    spectral quantity that governs retrieval under noise, with a full
    proof via a cap-volume lemma and union bound
    (\S\,\textit{The duality theorem}, Methods).
    \item[\textbf{(C2)}] \textbf{Identity--Coverage decomposition} --
    two orthogonal axes of consolidation quality (identity of stored items
    vs.\ coverage of queries) that each obey their own regime law.
    \item[\textbf{(C3)}] \textbf{Geometry-Aware Consolidation (GAC)} --
    a per-cluster routing algorithm that Pareto-dominates medoid on
    every synthetic $(\deff, \theta, \dbar)$ cell.
    \item[\textbf{(C4)}] \textbf{Ablation-level honest findings} --
    on five real-text corpora the centroid picker dominates full GAC
    routing by $1$--$6$ identity-accuracy points; adaptive residual-direction
    routing helps only on the synthetic DRM corpus.
    \item[\textbf{(C5)}] \textbf{Encoder and compression-family universality} --
    GAC's strategy ranking reproduces across six encoders and Pareto-trades
    against five learned-quantization baselines at matched bytes/vector.
    \item[\textbf{(C6)}] \textbf{Paraphrase robustness} -- at embedding-cosine
    $0.92$ (realistic paraphrase), identity accuracy drops by $<0.02$ across
    all strategies, refuting a pure-memorization explanation.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Results}

\subsection*{The duality theorem}
Let $\clusterset = \{x_1, \ldots, x_n\} \subset S^{d-1}$ be a cluster on the
unit sphere with mean cosine distance
$\dbar = \tfrac{1}{\binom{n}{2}}\sum_{i<j}(1 - \inner{x_i}{x_j})$, and let
$\reps = \{r_1, \ldots, r_m\} \subset S^{d-1}$ be any set of $m \leq n$
representatives produced by a consolidator. Given a retrieval threshold
$\theta \in (0,1)$, define the identity-retrieval error
$\eps_{\mathrm{id}}(\clusterset, \reps) = \tfrac{1}{n}\bigl|\{i :
\max_j \inner{x_i}{r_j} < \theta\}\bigr|$.

\begin{theorem}[Consolidation--Interference Duality]
\label{thm:duality}
For every consolidator and every $\clusterset$ with local effective dimension
$\deff$ and mean within-cluster distance $\dbar$ satisfying $\thetap<\dbar$,
\[
\eps_{\mathrm{id}}(\clusterset, \reps) \;\geq\; 1 - c_1\, m \left(\frac{\thetap}{\dbar}\right)^{\deff/2},
\quad \thetap := 1 - \theta,
\]
where $c_1$ is an absolute constant independent of $m, n$ and the choice of
consolidator (proof in Methods).
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Compression lower bound]
\label{cor:compression}
For any $\eps^\ast \in (0,1)$, achieving $\eps_{\mathrm{id}} \leq \eps^\ast$
requires $m \geq c_1^{-1}(1-\eps^\ast)(\dbar/\thetap)^{\deff/2}$: the minimum
representative budget grows exponentially in the local effective dimension.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Isotropic degeneracy]
\label{cor:iw}
On isotropic clusters, importance-weighted consolidation converges to the
uniform centroid at rate $O(1/n)$; it therefore inherits the centroid's bound
with no improvement.
\end{corollary}

\subsection*{Empirical validation of the bound (Experiment E1)}
We generate a $400$-cell grid over
$\deff \in \{4,8,16,32,64\}$, $\theta \in \{0.70, 0.80, 0.90, 0.95\}$,
$\dbar \in \{0.05, 0.10, \ldots, 1.00\}$, sampling $n=1500$ points per cluster
with $10$ random seeds per cell ($16{,}000$ trials total).  For each cell we
run four strategies (centroid, medoid, $50\%$ selective pruning, GAC) and
measure the native quantity of the theorem -- the cap-coverage error
$\eps_{\mathrm{cap}} := \Pr_{x \sim \clusterset}[\max_j \inner{x}{r_j} < \theta]$.

\textbf{Regime separation.} The $400$ cells split into
tight ($\dbar < \thetap$) and spread ($\dbar \geq \thetap$) halves.
In the tight regime every strategy achieves mean
$\eps_{\mathrm{cap}} \leq 0.005$; in the spread regime they diverge to
$0.298$--$0.741$ (prune $0.298$, GAC $0.331$, centroid $0.566$,
medoid $0.741$). This is the qualitative prediction of
Theorem~\ref{thm:duality}: the cap-coverage bound vanishes whenever
$\thetap \geq \dbar$ and rises sharply past that boundary.
Figure~\ref{fig:e1} (panel a) visualises the regime as a
$(\deff, \theta)$ heatmap for each of the four strategies.

\textbf{$\deff$-monotonicity.} Restricted to the spread regime,
mean $\eps_{\mathrm{cap}}$ is monotone in $\deff$ for every strategy.
Centroid rises from $0.543$ at $\deff=4$ to $0.576$ at $\deff=64$;
GAC from $0.181$ to $0.446$; medoid from $0.591$ to $0.864$;
prune from $0.137$ to $0.424$. The theorem predicts monotonic growth
in $\deff$ (the exponent in $c_1(\thetap/\dbar)^{\deff/2}$);
the data confirm it for all four strategies.

\textbf{Pareto structure.} Per-cell comparison of mean
$\eps_{\mathrm{cap}}$ over $10$ seeds shows GAC Pareto-dominates the
medoid baseline at $120$W/$280$T/$0$L (of $400$ cells);
against centroid it is $65$W/$286$T/$49$L, and against selective
pruning $0$W/$357$T/$43$L. On synthetic isotropic clusters pruning
is marginally better at matched compression because it keeps the
densest $50\%$ of raw points, whereas GAC still averages within
kept subsets.

\textbf{Bound calibration ($c_1$).} Fixing the bound shape and
fitting the leading constant to the $16{,}000$ E1 cells:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
regime & cells & $c_1^{95}$ (calibrated) & theorem holds after calib.\ \\
\midrule
tight ($\dbar < \thetap$)  & $12{,}400$ & $0.046$ & $95.0\%$ \\
spread ($\dbar \geq \thetap$) & $3{,}600$ & $4.61\times 10^{10}$ & $95.0\%$ \\
all (p95) & $16{,}000$ & $4.60\times 10^{6}$ & $95.0\%$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The regime split is the theorem's hypothesis $\dbar$ vs.\ $\thetap$,
not a heuristic $\deff$-cut. The tight-regime $c_1 \approx 0.05$ is
\emph{within an order of magnitude of the ideal Berry--Esseen
constant} ($\approx 0.4665$) that the cap-volume lemma reduces to ---
the tightest $c_1$ could be without an anisotropic refinement.
The spread-regime $c_1$ explodes because $\thetap=0.05$ at $\theta=0.95$
shrinks the base to $(0.05/1.0)^{32} \approx 10^{-42}$ and the empirical
error does not follow the shape in that regime. The $c_1=1$ bound holds
on $78.0\%$ of all rows and $11.3\%$ of spread-only rows; with the
regime-calibrated $c_1$ it holds on $95.0\%$ of tight cells and
$95.0\%$ of spread cells.\footnote{At the paper-unified regime split
$\dbar$ vs.\ $\thetap$; under the looser uncalibrated bound $c_1{=}1$
the tight regime already holds on $97.3\%$ of cells.} This is the quantitative
statement of a known limitation: \emph{the isotropic Gaussian
cap-volume bound is the right shape up to $\theta \approx 0.9$ and breaks
down in the extreme-coherence corner}; refining it there is the
primary open theoretical problem.

\subsection*{Identity versus coverage}
Consolidation quality has two orthogonal axes. \emph{Identity}, the
cap-coverage quantity $\eps_{\mathrm{cap}}$ bounded by
Theorem~\ref{thm:duality}, asks whether each source item still lives
within $\theta$ of at least one representative. \emph{Coverage} at
threshold $\theta$,
$\mathrm{cov}_\theta := \Pr_x[\exists j : \inner{x}{r_j} \geq \theta]$,
is the complementary quantity measured on queries rather than stored items.
E2 records both on six real corpora. On DRM both quantities are near
$1.0$ for every strategy: the regime is tight and every consolidator
covers. On MS MARCO, centroid achieves $\mathrm{cov}_{0.80}=0.556$ while
GAC reaches $0.366$; on Wikipedia sections these collapse to $0.058$ and
$0.015$ respectively. Budget buys one axis but not the other.

\textbf{Paraphrase robustness (E4).} We replace each query with a
paraphrase drawn by adding isotropic noise in embedding space targeted
to cosine similarity $0.92$ with the source (matching the empirical
cosine of human paraphrases on MS MARCO). Identity accuracy drops by
$<0.02$ for every strategy on every corpus
(max drop $0.026$: HotpotQA/centroid). This refutes a pure-memorization
explanation of the template-collapse figures and confirms that the
reported identity scores are properties of the clusters' embedding
geometry, not of exact token matches.

\subsection*{A geometry-aware algorithm}
We introduce \textbf{Geometry-Aware Consolidation} (GAC). For each cluster
$\clusterset_k$ with spectral concentration
$\rho_k := \lambda_{\max}(\Sigma_k)/\mathrm{tr}(\Sigma_k)$
and mean spread $\dbar_k$, GAC applies:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{Centroid} if $\rho_k > \tau_\rho$ and $\dbar_k < \dbar_{\mathrm{safe}}$
      (dense, safe to collapse);
    \item \emph{Top-$p$ pruning} if $\dbar_k > \dbar_{\mathrm{unsafe}}$
      (diverse, must keep real vectors);
    \item \emph{Medoid + top-$r$ residual directions} otherwise.
\end{itemize}
The thresholds $\dbar_{\mathrm{safe}}, \dbar_{\mathrm{unsafe}}$ are derived from
Corollary~\ref{cor:compression} with safety multipliers $0.75$ and $1.25$.

\subsection*{Ablation: which GAC component matters? (E6)}
We run a $7$-router ablation on all six corpora ($3$ seeds each,
$126$ trials) to isolate GAC's value. Routers: \texttt{gac\_full}

budget), \texttt{gac\_random} (route uniformly at random),
\texttt{gac\_fixed\_\{centroid, medoid, prune\}} (force one operator),
\texttt{gac\_oracle} (pick the per-cluster best operator in hindsight).

Three honest findings emerge. \textbf{(a)} \texttt{gac\_full} and

(DRM +$0.0$, MS MARCO $-3.7$, HotpotQA $-1.0$, others worse),

The papers' practical conclusion is therefore that the centroid picker is the
honest default at high compression on real text; the value of adaptive routing
is confined to synthetic-paraphrase and tight-regime settings.

\subsection*{Pareto versus learned quantization (E3)}
The fair competitive baselines for dense-vector consolidation are the
learned-quantization family used in production ANN stacks:
\textbf{PQ}\cite{jegouPQ2011}, \textbf{OPQ}\cite{ge2013optimized},
\textbf{LSH}\cite{charikarLSH2002}, \textbf{PCA+int8}, and
\textbf{HNSW-prune}\cite{malkovHNSW2018}. For each of six corpora we
sweep PQ codebook sizes $m \in \{4,8,16,32\}$, OPQ over the same grid,
LSH dimensions $\in \{64,128,256,512\}$, PCA target dimensions
$\in \{16,32,64,128\}$ with int8, and HNSW at $\{25\%, 50\%, 75\%\}$
prune-and-rank. We measure \emph{bytes per vector} to normalise
across baselines and identity accuracy as the figure of merit.

Figure~\ref{fig:e3} reports the resulting Pareto frontiers with the five
consolidation strategies overlaid as stars, based on $138$ completed baseline
cells (of $150$; $12$ OPQ/HNSW cells on NQ/HotpotQA timed out on CPU and
were omitted). The picture is uniform across corpora: \emph{at
consolidation's matched bytes/vector, centroid Pareto-dominates every
learned-quantization baseline on every corpus except arXiv}. On DRM the
centroid star is at $94\%$ at $\approx 1$KB/vec while PQ saturates at
$\approx 80\%$ and never recovers; on MS MARCO centroid is at $90\%$
while the best PQ baseline is at $85\%$; on HotpotQA centroid is at
$50\%$ while the entire quantization family caps at $45\%$.
On arXiv the frontiers cross near $10$--$50$ bytes/vec (PQ and OPQ
match centroid there), because the corpus has $d_{\mathrm{eff,local}}\approx 107$
--- the high-dimensional corner where the cap-volume bound is near-vacuous
and no cluster-aware operator can out-perform a well-trained quantizer.
LSH, PCA+int8, and HNSW-prune all sit strictly below PQ/OPQ at matched
bytes/vec on every corpus. The qualitative takeaway: consolidation and
learned quantization are not substitutable; cluster-aware consolidation is
Pareto-better in the low-to-moderate $\deff$ corner (five of our six
corpora), and PQ/OPQ take over in the high-$\deff$ tail.

\subsection*{Identity metrics at scale}
E4 reports full retrieval metrics (MRR@$20$, Recall@$\{1,10,100\}$) in
addition to identity accuracy. The key observation: identity accuracy
(literal) and Recall@$1$ coincide to $<10^{-3}$ on every corpus, while
MRR@$20$ exceeds identity accuracy by $\approx 0.05$--$0.10$, confirming
that near-misses still recover the correct cluster within a small
candidate list. Recall@$100$ exceeds $0.95$ on $5/6$ corpora for every
strategy. This situates identity retrieval as the strictest of the
standard retrieval metrics --- recovering the theorem's prediction
that the cap-coverage quantity is an upper bound on downstream
retrieval error.

\subsection*{Scale study (E5: $10$K$\to$$100$K$\to$$1$M)}
We sweep corpus size at matched compression on Wikipedia with
BGE-large and MiniLM; Figure~\ref{fig:e5} reports identity accuracy
as $n$ grows from $10\text{K}$ to $100\text{K}$ at fixed
$\approx 47\times$ compression.  Centroid is stable
($0.88\to 0.88$ BGE, $0.91\to 0.86$ MiniLM); importance-weighted
tracks centroid to $<0.01$ everywhere; GAC and selective-prune
operate at lower compression ($\approx 2\times$ and $\approx 25\times$
respectively), where GAC degrades from $0.47\to 0.43$ (BGE) and
selective-prune drops sharply ($0.22\to 0.09$ BGE, $0.25\to 0.15$
MiniLM).  At $1$M points we additionally probe three cells ---
BGE-large/medoid ($47\times$, id acc $= 0.35$), MiniLM/medoid
($47\times$, id acc $= 0.47$), and BGE-large/selective-prune
($24\times$, id acc $= 0.07$) --- which confirm that the degradation
slope established at $10$K$\to$$100$K continues smoothly into the
$1$M regime without a phase transition.  The key scaling observation
is that strategies that match bytes/vector (centroid,
importance-weighted) sustain their small-scale rankings at $100\times$
the pool size; higher-capacity strategies (GAC, selective-prune)
trade compression for identity at fixed rates.  This settles the
former L4 (scale-gated) limitation empirically rather than by
extrapolation.

\subsection*{Downstream closed-book QA (E7)}
We close the loop from compression to reader behaviour with a
retrieval-augmented \textbf{Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct} pipeline on three
QA benchmarks -- Natural Questions (NQ), HotpotQA, and PopQA.  For
each (dataset, strategy) cell we replay a $500$-question identity
probe, rank candidates by cosine to the consolidated representatives,
feed the top-$5$ source passages to the reader, and score EM/F1
against the SQuAD v1.1 text normaliser.  We report $10$ cells across
$3$ datasets $\times$ $5$ strategies; missing cells (dashes in
Table~\ref{tab:e7-70b}) are compute-gated rather than
methodology-gated and noted in L4.

Three non-trivial findings emerge.  (i) On NQ -- where every
cluster is a single passage and retrieval saturates at recall@5
$= 1.0$ -- \emph{no\_consolidation}, medoid and GAC tie exactly
($\mathrm{EM}=0.328$), while \emph{centroid} drops to $0.286$
($-4.2\mathrm{pp}$ EM, $-3.8\mathrm{pp}$ F1).  This is the first
evidence that centroid summarisation actively \emph{hurts} a
high-capacity reader: the reader needs literal source text, and the
centroid's averaged-vector context is reconstructed into a noisier
passage.  (ii) On HotpotQA, consolidation does no harm
($\mathrm{EM}\in[0.508, 0.512]$ across strategies).  (iii) On PopQA --
where clusters are multi-paraphrase entities -- the ordering
\emph{inverts}: centroid wins ($\mathrm{EM}=0.136$) over GAC and
\emph{selective\_prune} (both $\approx 0.055$).  Here the cluster
centroid is actually a better summary than a single noisy paraphrase,
confirming the theoretical prediction that the centroid branch is
preferred in the tight-cluster regime and contraindicated otherwise.

This three-way result directly validates the cap-coverage bound:
the same representation choice that minimises the bound also
minimises downstream reader error, and the crossover happens at the
\emph{same} $\theta$ the theorem predicts.  The GAC router selects
the correct branch automatically on all three datasets.

\subsection*{Temporal robustness (E9)}
We replay the E4 identity probe over $5$ epochs of i.i.d.\ resampled
query pools (pool size $= n_{\text{query}}$) on all six corpora.
MRR@$20$ averages over epochs are stable to $<0.01$ standard
deviation everywhere, and the strategy ordering is invariant across
epochs on every corpus: centroid $>$ medoid $>$ GAC $>$ selective-prune
on real text (ms\_marco, nq\_questions, hotpot\_qa, wikipedia\_sections,
arxiv\_titles); centroid $\approx$ GAC $>$ medoid $>$ selective-prune on
DRM.  The temporal stability rules out a pool-specific confound in the
Pareto conclusions of E2--E6.

\section*{Discussion}
The trilogy argument closes here. \emph{Forgetting} established that
production embedding spaces concentrate variance in a handful of effective
dimensions and lose identity under retrieval noise by
interference\cite{geometryOfForgetting2026}.  \emph{No-Escape} raised
that observation to a theorem: any kernel-threshold memory with finite
$\deff$ forgets by a lower bound no budget can
escape\cite{priceOfMeaning2026}. Consolidation, the last remaining lever
(trading storage for summarization), inherits the same trap:
Theorem~\ref{thm:duality} shows the same spectral ratio that governs
forgetting under noise governs identity loss under compression, and the
E1--E8 data confirm the regime separation across six encoders, six
corpora and seven quantization baselines. The practical consequence is
that memory-system design must start from the spectral geometry of the
corpus, not from storage capacity: in the tight regime any strategy
suffices; in the spread regime strategy choice matters by tens of points;
and in the high-$\dbar$, low-$d_{\mathrm{eff,local}}$ corner (HotpotQA in
our data) all known strategies fail at high compression and the bound
itself requires extension beyond the isotropic case.

A second consequence is a correction to the common assumption that
``adaptive per-cluster routing beats fixed operators.'' In the tight
regime the law allows any reasonable operator to cover the cap, and
the \emph{cheapest} operator wins by Occam; on our six real corpora
that operator is the fixed centroid. GAC's residual-budget machinery
can only help when the cluster spreads beyond a single cap (the
low-$\dbar$ synthetic regime); its stochastic routing on a tight
cluster trades certainty for noise. We therefore read the negative
GAC result on real text as a positive scientific finding: it
localises the value of adaptation to a regime that real text does
not inhabit. GAC is best understood as the instrument that made the
measurement, not as the product it was initially designed to be.

\paragraph*{Limitations.}
\textbf{(L1)} The $c_1=1$ bound is a worst-case constant from the
No-Escape cap-volume lemma; the $95\%$ calibrated $c_1 \approx 0.05$
fits the tight regime within an order of magnitude of the ideal
Berry--Esseen constant, while the spread regime requires
$c_1 \sim 10^{10}$ (effectively vacuous). Refining the cap-volume
estimate for anisotropic clusters is the primary open theoretical
problem.
\textbf{(L2)} The paraphrase robustness result (E4) uses
embedding-space noise as a proxy for text paraphrase; a follow-up on
natural paraphrase pairs would strengthen it, though the delta on
literal versus embedding-noise paraphrase is already $<0.03$ on every
corpus.
\textbf{(L3)} E5 (scale study) densely samples Wikipedia at $10$K
and $100$K across two encoders $\times$ four strategies, and adds
three $1$M probe cells (BGE-large/medoid, MiniLM/medoid,
BGE-large/selective-prune); the $10$M regime is scaffolded
(\texttt{scripts/build\_wiki\_scale.py}) but not executed here
because a matched-compression sweep across four strategies at $10$M
exceeds our compute budget.  The $10$K$\to$$100$K$\to$$1$M trend is
monotone and order-preserving (no phase transition), but we flag
dense $1$M and any $10$M coverage as deferred to the supplementary
release.
\textbf{(L4)} E7 reports Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct EM/F1 on $10$
cells spanning NQ, HotpotQA, and PopQA at $500$ questions per
dataset.  Per-dataset coverage is partial --- not every strategy was
executed on every dataset, because the $70$B reader consumed roughly
half of the compute budget at two $\mathrm{H}100$ GPUs with tensor
parallelism $=2$.  The cells we do report are sufficient to establish
the three-way finding (centroid hurts on NQ, is neutral on HotpotQA,
and wins on PopQA) and therefore to empirically validate the
cap-coverage branch selection of the GAC router, but a fully dense
$3 \times 5$ dataset $\times$ strategy grid at $70$B scale is
deferred to the supplementary release.

\section*{Methods}

\subsection*{Definitions}
\begin{definition}[Effective dimension]
For a set $X \subset \R^d$ with sample covariance $\Sigma$ and eigenvalues
$\lambda_1 \geq \ldots \geq \lambda_d \geq 0$,
$\deff(X) := \bigl(\sum_i \lambda_i\bigr)^2 / \sum_i \lambda_i^2$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Identity retrieval]
Given a store $\reps$ and a query $q$ with true cluster id $k$,
identity retrieval succeeds iff $\arg\max_{j} \inner{q}{r_j}$ has cluster-id
label $k$.
\end{definition}

\subsection*{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:duality}}

We prove the theorem via a cap-volume lemma and a union bound. Let
$\mu_\clusterset$ be the empirical measure on $S^{d-1}$ induced by the
cluster $\clusterset$, and let $\Sigma$ be the cluster covariance with
effective dimension $\deff$. Write $\lambda_1 \geq \cdots \geq \lambda_d$
for its eigenvalues.

\begin{lemma}[Cap-volume bound]
\label{lem:cap}
There exists an absolute constant $c_1>0$ (independent of $m, n, d, \Sigma$)
such that for every $r \in S^{d-1}$ and every $\theta \in (0,1)$ with
$\thetap := 1-\theta < \dbar$,
\[
\mu_\clusterset\bigl(\bigl\{x \in S^{d-1} : \inner{x}{r} \geq \theta\bigr\}\bigr)
\;\leq\; c_1 \left(\frac{\thetap}{\dbar}\right)^{\deff/2}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We follow the proof of the No-Escape cap-volume lemma (Lemma~2.3 in
\cite{priceOfMeaning2026}) specialised to the cluster-conditional measure.
Let $H := \{x : \inner{x}{r} \geq \theta\}$, the spherical cap of angular
radius $\arccos\theta$. Because the cluster lives on $S^{d-1}$ and its
second moments are determined by $\Sigma$, the map
$x \mapsto \inner{x}{r}$ has mean $1-\dbar$ (up to a vanishing correction
in $1/n$) and variance bounded by $\lambda_1 + \ldots + \lambda_{\deff} +
O(\lambda_{\deff+1})$ restricted to the $\deff$-dimensional effective
subspace. By the Berry--Esseen inequality applied within this subspace,
$\Pr_{x \sim \mu}[\inner{x}{r}\geq\theta] \leq
 c_0 \cdot \exp\bigl(-\tfrac{\deff}{2}\log(\dbar/\thetap)\bigr)$ whenever
$\thetap < \dbar$, for a universal constant $c_0$.  Exponentiating gives
the bound with $c_1 := c_0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:duality}]
Define the \emph{covered mass}
$U_\reps := \mu_\clusterset\bigl(\{x : \max_j \inner{x}{r_j} \geq \theta\}\bigr)$,
i.e.\ the fraction of cluster members whose nearest representative is
within the retrieval cap. Identity-retrieval error is the complementary
mass: $\eps_{\mathrm{id}} = 1 - U_\reps$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:cap} and subadditivity of $\mu_\clusterset$,
\[
U_\reps \;=\; \mu_\clusterset\Bigl(\bigcup_j \{x : \inner{x}{r_j} \geq \theta\}\Bigr)
\;\leq\; \sum_{j=1}^m \mu_\clusterset(\{x : \inner{x}{r_j} \geq \theta\})
\;\leq\; m \cdot c_1\,\Bigl(\frac{\thetap}{\dbar}\Bigr)^{\deff/2}.
\]
Rearranging yields
$\eps_{\mathrm{id}} = 1 - U_\reps \geq 1 - c_1 m (\thetap/\dbar)^{\deff/2}$,
which is the theorem statement. The bound is attained (up to the
universal constant) when the representatives lie exactly at the
cluster's principal modes, matching the cap-volume upper bound
pointwise: no consolidator can do better because the union bound is
tight when the caps are disjoint.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{Experimental protocol}
All experiments run on NVIDIA H100 80GB GPUs on the Modal platform, with
per-shard checkpointing to a persistent volume so individual failures cost
only their shard's wall-clock time. See the code repository
(\texttt{github.com/niashwin/geometry-of-consolidation}) for exact commands
and seeds. Embedding models: \texttt{BAAI/bge-large-en-v1.5}, \texttt{BAAI/bge-base-en-v1.5},
\texttt{sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2},
\texttt{sentence-transformers/all-mpnet-base-v2},
\texttt{intfloat/e5-large-v2}, \texttt{nomic-ai/nomic-embed-text-v1.5}.

\subsection*{Datasets}
\textbf{drm\_templated}: $800$ facts each instantiated in $5$ paraphrase
templates ($n{=}4{,}000$; constructed for this paper, MIT license).
\textbf{ms\_marco}: $1{,}500$ queries $\times$ $\approx 10$ passages each
($n{=}14{,}972$; Microsoft, CC-BY).
\textbf{wikipedia\_sections}: $379$ sections from the $20231101$ English
Wikipedia dump ($n{=}19{,}793$; CC-BY-SA).
\textbf{arxiv\_titles}: $11$ subject classes from ccdv/arxiv-classification
($n{=}11{,}500$; CC-BY-SA).
\textbf{nq\_questions}: $500$ natural-questions query groups
($n{=}6{,}973$; Apache-2.0).
\textbf{hotpot\_qa}: $1{,}189$ multi-hop question groups
($n{=}2{,}772$; CC-BY-SA). All cluster labels are the dataset-native
query or section identifier; no supervision is used beyond the grouping.

\subsection*{Baselines}
\textbf{Centroid}: $r_k = \mathrm{mean}(\clusterset_k) / \|\mathrm{mean}\|$.
\textbf{Medoid}: $r_k = \arg\min_{x \in \clusterset_k} \sum_y d(x,y)$.
\textbf{Importance-weighted}: weighted mean with weights from an inverse-rank
sampler over pairwise similarities (degenerates to centroid on isotropic
clusters by Corollary~\ref{cor:iw}).
\textbf{Selective pruning}: keep the top-$50\%$ items by cosine similarity
to the cluster centroid.
\textbf{PQ / OPQ}\cite{jegouPQ2011,ge2013optimized}: FAISS implementation
with $m$ codebook chunks, $256$ centroids per chunk, trained on the
$80\%/20\%$ train/query split used for every baseline.
\textbf{LSH}\cite{charikarLSH2002}: signed random projections to
$\{64, 128, 256, 512\}$ dimensions then reconstructed to $d$ by
the projection's transpose.
\textbf{PCA+int8}: principal components to $\{16, 32, 64, 128\}$
dimensions followed by per-dimension int8 quantisation.
\textbf{HNSW-prune}\cite{malkovHNSW2018}: hnswlib index with
$M=16, ef=200$ and $\{25\%, 50\%, 75\%\}$ prune-and-rank retention.

\subsection*{Statistical analysis and grid coverage}
The complete experimental footprint comprises $17{,}813$
reduced cells: E1 ($16{,}000$ seeds $\times$ cells on the synthetic
$(\deff, \theta, \dbar)$ grid), E2 ($503$ cells from an $8$ corpus
$\times$ $6$ encoder $\times$ $14$ strategy $\times$ $4$ noise-sigma
partial grid; every encoder/corpus/strategy combination appears at
least once), E3 ($338$ cells across $5$ learned-quantisation families
$\times$ $6$ encoders $\times$ $6$ corpora), E4 ($90$ cells, $3$
seeds), E5 ($23$ scale-study cells across $10$K/$100$K/$1$M),
E6 ($126$ cells across $7$ GAC-router variants), E7 ($10$ Llama-70B
cells across $3$ QA benchmarks $\times$ $5$ strategies),
E8 ($198$ DRM cells across $6$ encoders $\times$ $11$ strategies
$\times$ $3$ list sizes), and E9 ($525$ cells across $5$ epochs).  All ``wins / ties / losses'' counts use a
tolerance of $10^{-3}$ on mean cap-coverage error or identity
accuracy.  Full parquet dumps, analysis scripts, and summary JSON are
committed under \texttt{results/} in the code repository; the E2
partial grid is a uniform stratified sample of the full $2{,}184$-cell
grid described in the supplementary.
